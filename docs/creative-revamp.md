# Creative Revamp — Proposed Direction

**Issue:** #28  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Working title:** **Panelhouse**  
**Owning repository:** InkTime until an approved rename is executed

## Purpose

The renewed persistent-IP design fixed the game's domain direction, but the project still carries too much identity from its historical jam-oriented comic-studio / era-management origin.

The next phase should not merely reskin that structure.

The project needs a distinct player fantasy, world framing, public title, art direction, interaction language, and emotional contract while preserving the principles that still make the renewed design valuable.

This document is a **creative-direction proposal**, not proof of implementation and not approval of a technical rename.

## Source constraints

This proposal follows current YFramework and Loomlight Game Studio doctrine:

- independent games own their own world, genre, emotional contract, and dominant art direction;
- Polite Goblin's recurring signature should be thoughtful authorship, useful creativity, play, and considered detail rather than one mandatory visual style;
- familiarity should provide an entry point without becoming the game's identity;
- define a desirable player dream, authorial hook, anti-patterns, and smallest validation artifact before scaling production;
- attractive visuals are insufficient if they do not strengthen playable invitation, relationship, and delivered experience.

Loomlight Game Studio is used here as an orchestration/review lens. It does not own the game's design.

## Quick concept pass

### Dream sold

> Run a strange publishing house where every fictional world you create keeps living after the page.

### One-line hook

> **Publish worlds that remember what became canon.**

### Player role

The player is the founder, editor, and creative director of an unusual publishing house.

The fantasy is not “manage comic employees efficiently” and not “use software to generate a comic.” The fantasy is **bringing fictional worlds into existence, committing them to publication, and living with what those worlds remember**.

### Authorial philosophy

Creative choices matter because they persist.

A story becomes more meaningful when later work must acknowledge what the player previously established, changed, damaged, promised, or left unresolved.

### Principal pillar

**Feeling — creative attachment.**

The dominant target is attachment to authored fictional worlds and recurring characters.

Seeing and Doing support that feeling:

- **Seeing:** created worlds gain visible presence and history;
- **Doing:** editing, arranging, committing, and publishing change persistent state.

### Soul rule

**Published choices leave visible, persistent traces.**

If publication becomes only export, if generated material is disposable, or if characters reset between works, the game has lost its identity.

### Dramatic engine

Every new publication creates opportunity and constraint.

The player wants to make something new, but previous canon, character relationships, unresolved consequences, and authored commitments make each next work more specific.

This turns continuity from database maintenance into creative pressure.

## Core loop

`Imagine world -> establish people/canon -> shape a story -> stage it on living pages -> commit/publish -> see persistent consequences -> return to a changed world -> create again`

The critical shift is:

> **Publication is a commitment with continuity consequences, not merely the end of a production pipeline.**

## Art direction — Living Editorial Diorama

### Immediate read

A tactile, unusual publishing environment in which pages, panels, proofs, and editorial objects have physical depth and fictional material can visibly escape or exceed the page.

The game should read first as an authored fictional place, not as an office simulator or desktop application.

### Material language

Use a controlled mixture of:

- paper and card;
- black and coloured inks;
- halftone / screenprint / risograph texture;
- translucent vellum;
- tape, clips, staples, stamps, crop marks, annotations, printing plates, proofs and registration marks;
- cut edges and layered shallow depth;
- occasional glossy, metallic, fluorescent, or “wet ink” accents where they carry meaning.

Print-process references should feel contemporary and intentional. They must not become nostalgia for old production limitations.

### Spatial language

The preferred game-facing presentation is **2.5D / shallow-diorama editorial space** rather than a flat creator-tool UI.

Useful motifs:

- pages unfolding into small spatial scenes;
- panel borders acting as masks, doors, stages, or compositional frames;
- characters or props crossing gutters;
- layers sliding, folding, pinning, stamping, tearing, binding, or registering;
- published work accumulating physically in an archive;
- subtle world-specific motion or ambience bleeding out of an active page.

This does **not** imply freely explorable portal worlds.

### Shape and composition

- strong macro-shapes before micro-detail;
- readable silhouettes;
- large intentional framing devices;
- asymmetric editorial composition;
- detail concentrated around current attention;
- negative space used deliberately;
- clear visual hierarchy at actual gameplay scale.

### Colour

The Panelhouse wrapper should have a recognizable neutral/material base, while active fictional IPs may introduce controlled spot-colour systems.

This allows the game to own a stable identity without forcing every player-created comic to share the same palette.

Prefer expressive colour allocation over all-over saturation.

### Motion

Motion should feel editorial and material:

- page turns;
- registration shifts snapping into place;
- ink spreading/drying;
- panels expanding;
- paper layers lifting;
- annotations appearing;
- archive objects accumulating;
- small character motions acknowledging edits or publication.

Avoid decorative motion that competes with reading.

### Sound

The meta-world can use tactile production sounds:

- paper;
- pencil;
- nib;
- ink roller;
- tape;
- clips;
- stapler;
- cutter;
- press/register mechanisms;
- page handling.

Fictional IPs may add restrained ambience that leaks through active pages, reinforcing that worlds continue beyond the current frame.

## Two visual layers

### 1. Panelhouse meta-world

Fixed project identity.

It owns:

- the publishing-space visual language;
- navigation;
- editorial interactions;
- archive presentation;
- continuity feedback;
- UI framing;
- meta-world motion/audio.

### 2. Player-created publications

Variable per fictional IP.

A player should be able to create an absurd comedy, noir mystery, fantasy adventure, romance, horror comic, or another supported style without making the entire game wrapper incoherent.

The wrapper should frame and present those works rather than flatten them into one mandatory comic style.

## Relationship promise

Persistent characters should gradually feel less like fields in a database and more like recurring creative companions.

Useful moments include:

- a character visually reappearing because an old relationship matters;
- a prior canon fact resurfacing during a new story;
- a page/proof retaining a visible mark from an important publication;
- an archive object reminding the player of an earlier commitment;
- a continuity warning framed as a meaningful creative tension rather than an error dialog.

## Authorship hook

The strongest differentiator is not “AI can make comics.”

It is:

> **The fictional world continues off-panel and remembers what the player published.**

Manual, AI, and Hybrid authorship all serve this same fantasy.

AI remains a proposal-making tool behind game-owned contracts. It is never the authorial authority or the game identity.

## Second-look rewards

The visual direction should reward inspection with evidence of history rather than arbitrary clutter:

- old proof marks;
- crossed-out titles;
- character notes;
- taped repairs;
- prior editions;
- annotations from past decisions;
- physical wear around repeatedly used material;
- small archive changes after continuity updates;
- relationships implied by where artifacts cluster or overlap.

## Anti-patterns

### Retired identity

Do not return to:

- fixed 1980–2030 progression as the main fantasy;
- employee/Creativity management as the core loop;
- a one-room production-office identity;
- first-print nostalgia/economy as the emotional hook;
- fixed-three-panel strips as the permanent foundation;
- the historical Main scene as visual target.

### Attractive failures to avoid

- **Generic cozy stationery game:** warm desk, plants, notebooks, soft colours, little authored specificity.
- **Creator software disguised as a game:** forms, panels and inspectors become the main experience.
- **Game Dev Story with comics:** employees, stations and throughput dominate the fantasy.
- **AI comic generator:** generation is the headline verb and authored continuity is secondary.
- **Retro print nostalgia:** halftone/risograph become historical cosplay instead of a contemporary visual system.
- **Generic superhero language:** speech bubbles, POW shapes and comic clichés substitute for project identity.
- **Polite Goblin game skin:** company mascot/palette becomes the game's dominant art direction.
- **Portal-world overpromise:** imagery suggests broad free exploration that the actual product does not deliver.
- **Unreadable skeuomorphism:** tactile presentation harms accessibility, text clarity, navigation, or editing efficiency.

## What survives

The following current principles remain strong unless later evidence falsifies them:

- persistent IP identity;
- stable identifiers;
- persistent Characters, Relationships, and Canon;
- player-reviewed continuity;
- Manual / AI / Hybrid through one canonical structured script;
- provider-independent AI boundaries;
- shared publication structures for Strip and Comic;
- page/panel composition;
- digital PDF export downstream of structured publication data;
- publication history;
- Loomlight Flux as reusable framework rather than game identity.

PR #27's minimum persistent IP model is directionally compatible with this revamp.

## What should pause

Do not let #6 onward harden presentation assumptions from the old game.

Domain work may continue only where it remains presentation-agnostic.

Before investing heavily in UI, scenes, production presentation, content pipelines, character presentation, or publication composition, validate the new creative wrapper.

## Smallest validation artifact

Build one compact **Panelhouse desk/diorama proof** containing:

- one persistent IP;
- one character;
- one relationship or canon fact;
- one page with one or a few panels;
- one tactile edit interaction;
- one publish/commit action;
- one visible persistent change after publication;
- one archive/history trace.

The proof should test:

1. Does the game immediately read as a fictional publishing world rather than creator software?
2. Does the IP feel as if it exists beyond its current page?
3. Is publication emotionally legible as a commitment?
4. Can one persistent wrapper comfortably present a visually different fictional IP?
5. Does the tactile language remain readable and accessible?
6. Is the result materially more distinctive than the historical InkTime framing?

## Naming

### Recommended working title

**Panelhouse**

Why it fits:

- “Panel” anchors comic language without reducing the game to strips.
- “House” works simultaneously as publishing house, creative home, and inhabited place.
- It supports the visual idea of a physical editorial world.
- It is compact enough to function as a project identity while the deeper dream is explained by capsule/trailer language.

This is a **working title only**. It requires naming/trademark/domain/storefront checks before commercial commitment.

### Alternatives considered

**Continuity Bureau**  
Strong on systemic persistence and procedural humour, but risks pushing the game back toward office/bureaucratic-management framing.

**Print Atelier / zine-first identity**  
Strong visual craft and feasible production language, but risks making print nostalgia the product instead of persistent fictional worlds.

The recommended direction therefore treats print/editorial craft as the **material language**, not the core fantasy.

## Technical naming boundary

Do not combine creative approval with a broad code rename.

Until the title is approved:

- repository may remain `InkTime`;
- namespaces/assemblies may remain `InkThroughTime.*`;
- existing save directory names may remain;
- serialized types should not be renamed merely for branding.

After title approval, create a separate bounded rename plan covering repository/display name, product name, Unity Player settings, namespaces/assemblies if worth changing, folders, save migration, serialized compatibility, documentation, agents, and YFramework routing.

## YFramework synchronization

YFramework currently records the accepted InkTime persistent comic-IP direction and correctly routes implementation authority to this repository.

Do not edit that record merely because this proposal exists.

After the owning game repository accepts the new title and creative direction, synchronize YFramework so cross-repository terminology no longer points collaborators back to the retired InkTime identity.
